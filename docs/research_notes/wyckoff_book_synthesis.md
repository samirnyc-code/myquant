# Wyckoff Methodology (Villahermosa, *The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth*) — synthesis for our tools

Source: `638179589-2-Ruben-Villahermosa-The-Wyckoff-Methodology-in-Depth.pdf` (276 pp), read
in full by 6 parallel agents (2026-09-15). Page numbers are the book's own. This note drives
the TempoSpeedometer / TR-zone tool rebuild.

## 0. The disputes this settles (why our marks were "terrible")

- **The range-creating low is the CLIMAX (Phase A), NOT a spring.** "its appearance allows us to
  define the limits of the range; because the lowest price marks the lowest end of the structure"
  (p.78). We were labelling the climax low as a spring.
- **A Spring is a Phase-C event and is defined RETROACTIVELY:** "a false breakout can only be
  labeled a Spring or an Upthrust After Distribution when it originates the movement that breaks
  the structure. The Spring must necessarily cause the [opposite-side] breakout of the range...
  Anything other than this... is simply a test." (p.105-106). Also: "a real Spring is automatically
  followed by a breakout movement" (p.258).
- **Phase-C timing gate:** only hunt the spring once "Phase B has exceeded the time spent in
  Phase A" (p.105). A low before that is too early to be a spring.
- **Test = No Supply / No Demand:** "a bullish/bearish candlestick with a narrow range and lower
  volume than the previous two candles" (p.96). A test does not break a level or launch the trend.
- **Focus on dynamics, not labels:** "it is not... a question of identifying structures, events and
  phases to the millimeter as if we were robots" (p.250); "focus on... price dynamics and not... labels" (p.169).

## 1. The trading range as a BOX (edges + phases)

- Box edges: **Creek** = accumulation resistance = "the high generated by the Automatic Rally and
  any highs during Phase B" (p.147). **Ice** = distribution support = "the low generated by the
  Automatic Reaction and any lows during Phase B" (p.150). The OPPOSITE edge = the climax extreme
  (SC low / BC high) (p.78, 84).
- Edges are ZONES not lines; false breaks can hit the outer edge OR interior minor highs/lows
  (p.47, 53). Tolerance is expected: "even if this means drawing a zone with a fairly wide area
  rather than a thin line" (p.154). ← matches the user's wick→body zone method.
- **Phases A-E** (p.134-144): A = stop (PS·SC/BC·AR·ST; ends at ST). B = build cause (UA/UT,
  ST-as-SOW/mSOW). C = test (Spring/UTAD false break). D = trend within range (SOS/SOW + LPS/LPSY).
  E = trend outside range. **Phase B should be longer than A and C** (p.137); a short B = urgency/
  stronger ensuing move.
- Sloping ranges: draw one limit through A/B/C extremes, clone to the other side, "ensure the
  channel contains practically all of the price action" (p.154). Slope up = strength, down = weakness (p.153, 264).

## 2. Events — exact criteria (for detection)

- **Climax (SC/BC):** culmination after a long move; the extreme that defines one range edge. Volume
  EITHER a wide-range spike OR "a set of bars with... high and constant volume" (p.75). Confirmed
  retroactively by AR+ST (p.74). Can end by **exhaustion** with LOW/declining volume too (p.78-79).
- **AR:** big move opposite the climax; sets the other edge; volume high at start then declines (p.83).
- **ST:** narrowing ranges + volume lower than the climax (p.90); classify by which third of the range
  it ends in — mid=neutral, upper=buyer imbalance, lower=seller imbalance (p.90, 195).
- **Spring / UTAD (Phase C):** false break of the established edge that MUST cause the breakout
  (retroactive). Three types (p.107-109):
  - **#1 Terminal Shakeout** — deep penetration, high volume, wide range; must be tested.
  - **#2** — moderate penetration/volume/range; must be tested.
  - **#3** — shallow penetration, LOW volume, narrow range; tradable immediately, no test needed.
  - **minor spring** — stays inside the range (doesn't exceed the edge) (p.109).
- **Spring test:** narrowing ranges, decreasing volume, must stay ABOVE the spring low (p.110).
- **SOS/SOW (Phase D breakout):** wide range + high volume, price holds beyond the level; the
  SOS/SOWbar (wide body, close in final third, higher volume) is the entry trigger (p.119-121, 231).
- **LPS/BUEC, LPSY/FTI (Phase D/E confirmation):** low-volume, narrow-range break-and-retest that
  does not re-enter (p.124). Wyckoff's favourite entry (p.225).

## 3. THE tick-vs-time answer

- The book's "volume" = **contracts traded** (p.41), always read **RELATIVELY** (vs prior bars /
  prior wave / prior impulse), never with absolute thresholds. On a 2000-tick bar, only *trade count*
  is fixed — *contract volume* still varies, so volume is not dead on tick, just noisier.
- **The unlock is the Weis Wave:** "the accumulation of transactions made per price movement...
  identify the start and end point of a price movement... add up all the volume traded during the
  development of that movement and represent it in the form of waves" (p.203). It is volume PER SWING,
  not per bar → **bar-type agnostic; works identically on tick and time.** A swing spans a variable
  number of tick bars, recovering the effort signal per-bar volume loses on fixed-tick.
- P&F is the one construction the book calls explicitly "based on volatility... unlike bar charts,
  which are time-based" (p.39) — precedent for non-time analysis.
- **Recommendation:** measure effort on SWINGS (Weis-wave), not bars → one engine serves 2k-tick and
  time charts. Keep tempo (ticks/sec) as a complementary rate at the breakout bar (the one spot tick
  is weakest). Range (spread) is undistorted on tick and the book weights it heavily — use it.

## 4. Multi-timeframe

- Analyse **top-down for context** (weekly→daily→intraday): "start... on a higher time frame and
  from there go down" (p.206); "until we are clear about this context... we cannot start to look at
  the shorter time frame" (p.207).
- Identify **bottom-up**: "prioritize the development of structures in shorter time frames" for initial
  identification (p.210).
- **Nesting:** a minor structure AT a macro liquidity zone IS the macro event (a minor accumulation at
  a macro Spring zone = the macro Spring) (p.208-212). This is how a 2k-tick micro-range becomes the
  execution trigger for a daily-chart Wyckoff event.
- So: draw ranges/context on a TIME chart (daily/H1); execute the nested micro-structure on 2k-tick.
  The tools must run on both.

## 5. Trading logic (overlays)

- Only trade Phase **C / D / E** (p.221). Best R/R = Phase C. Wyckoff's favourite = the BUEC/LPS
  break-and-retest (p.225).
- Entry triggers: the **spring test** (narrow, low vol, holds above spring low) or the **SOS/SOWbar**
  (p.223, 231). Stops: below the spring low / below the SOSbar (long); mirror for short (p.234-235).
  Targets: opposite edge → liquidity zones (prior SC/AR lows, BC/AR highs), VPOC/VWAP; exit fully on
  an ultra-high-volume opposing climax or the opposing structure's Phase A/C (p.238-243).
- Context lock: once the higher-TF reads accumulation, only surface LONGs (p.215-216).

## 6. Proposed rebuild (what to actually build)

1. **"Wyckoff TR Box" drawing tool** (whole range, not a thin zone): user draws the box; tool
   auto-partitions Phase A-E by event/time, labels events, draws Creek/Ice edge-zones (wick→body).
   Edges are zones with tolerance (p.154).
2. **Corrected event engine** (state machine, retroactive):
   - Climax = range-creating extreme (Phase A) — labelled, never a spring.
   - Spring/UTAD candidate = false break of the edge in Phase C (after B>A) → PROMOTE to confirmed
     only when price breaks the opposite edge (SOS/SOW); else downgrade to test. Show "SP?" then "SP".
   - Test = No Supply/No Demand (narrow range + volume < prior 2 swings/bars).
   - Spring type (#1/#2/#3) by penetration depth + volume + range.
3. **Weis-wave effort engine** (swing volume) → tick+time parity; drives climax/test/SOS grading.
4. **Trade overlay:** entry (spring-test / SOSbar / BUEC), stop, targets per §5.
5. **Multi-timeframe:** same tools on time charts for context; nested execution on 2k.

## 7. Standing cautions from the book

- Don't over-label ("robots", p.250); grade by dynamics.
- Not every range is accumulation/distribution — some are noise (p.28, 36); gate before annotating.
- The author warns specifically against "a 1 or 2 minute chart full of labels" (p.214) — i.e. our
  spray problem is a known failure mode. Fewer, confirmed, phase-gated marks.
