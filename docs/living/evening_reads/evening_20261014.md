## 5. Strategy Implications: Negative Gamma Requires Directional Structures, Not Income

The regime change from positive to negative gamma has direct and material implications for structure selection. **Income structures are the wrong tool for this environment.** In negative gamma, dealers are short options and must buy into rallies and sell into dips to hedge their exposure — this amplifies moves and invalidates the range-bound, mean-reverting premise that iron condors and credit spreads require to function.

**Appropriate structures for a negative gamma, elevated-VIX environment:**

- **Directional debit spreads (put verticals):** If the PPI/Retail Sales complex comes in hot and continues the CPI repricing narrative, defined-risk bearish structures targeting S1 (7,678) and the Put Wall (7,670) as downside objectives are structurally aligned. The negative gamma feedback loop supports trending continuation rather than reversal.
- **Long puts or put verticals with defined risk:** With VIX at 16.87, premium is elevated but not extreme. Long premium structures in a negative gamma environment benefit from dealer hedging amplification, which increases realized volatility — the core condition under which long gamma positions outperform.
- **Tactical call spreads on a relief bounce:** If PPI surprises to the downside and SPX recovers toward R1 (7,774) / Call Wall (7,800), a short-dated call spread capped at the call wall captures the rally within a defined risk framework, with the call wall acting as a natural ceiling. Be precise on the cap — the call wall is a hard resistance in negative gamma as dealer selling into rallies does not disappear.
- **Avoid undefined-risk short vol:** Short strangles, naked puts, or short condors are explicitly wrong-sided in a negative gamma regime. Dealer flows amplify rather than suppress, and the macro catalyst stack at 08:30 introduces binary gap risk.
