**In short:** Gold (GC) and crude (CL) are the same measured-move method on "monster" charts — huge tick ranges, so they DIP DEEP into their 50% retracements and fake you out. The signature trade is the **second test / "gotcha" trade**: the first test gets shaken out (a failed breakout leaving a hammer wick), and only THEN does the real move begin. Never over-leverage — these are the most volatile, most dangerous instruments; there is no TICK indicator here.

## Contracts & sizing (the #1 rule: don't over-leverage)
- GC = 100 oz, $10/tick; CL = 1,000 bbl, $10/tick. Minis: YG (~33 oz, $3.32/tick) for gold; for crude, Nadex/binary options give ~$1/tick exposure.
- Max **1% risk/trade**; **0.5% on Mondays/Fridays** or not at all. Over-leverage → emotion → bad decisions. There are enough small instruments to size correctly; there's no excuse.
- Free-trade mechanics: stop ≤12 ticks (tighten only), front-run +4 (limit), first target +6, then stop → −6.

## Best / worst times
- **GC:** trade the Euro-open (3am ET) → Euro close; sweet spot 8:00–11:30 ET (Euro+NY overlap). Asian session is active but low-volume/violent — better to HOLD a position through Asia than open a new one.
- **CL:** Euro-open → 2:30pm ET pit close (2:30 = big volatility). Best 8:00–11:30. After 2:30 it's dead-quiet till 3am — dangerous.
- **11:30am = the reversal / doldrums** for both. After Euro close: manage positions, don't open new ones (or 0.5% risk). Post-close moves are pure market-order reactions, then straight back to the slog.

## The second test = the whole method here
- Normal progression: first test touches (limit front-run of 4 → very high % of first targets hit) → **but it gets shaken out** → price retests and dips in (the second/"dangerous" test) → reverses off a long wick (**hammer** in a long, inverted hammer in a short) → series begins toward target.
- Unlike the indices (where a second test usually breaks straight through), on GC/CL the second test is a **failed breakout — a "gotcha" trade** that traps breakout-buyers at highs / breakdown-sellers at lows. That trapped fuel IS what powers the move to target. "It's almost as if the dip-in is needed to complete the target."
- **Tell that a second test is still coming: an unbroken OPPOSING measured move leading price back in.** If the opposing long hasn't broken, don't trust the halfway-back short — you'll get run over on the retest.

## Entry priority (after you see second-test participation)
1. **Front-run the participation** — front-run the traded high (stop above) short / low (stop below) long. Big ranges mean this sometimes needs ~12 ticks of room. You're watching the hammer form = the signal.
2. **Micros — dangerous, avoid on CL.** They break almost every time because the prior high/low gets retested repeatedly ("everything trades twice, at least"). Only trust micros AFTER the opposing series breaks. Gold's micros are more technical/tradeable than CL's.
3. **Series of 15-min measured moves** — only trade the series AFTER the second test has traded and the opposing measured move has broken (i.e. after a double-bottom/double-top forms). Never trade the series coming out of the FIRST test — it runs back into the second and stops you.

## Taking profit
- Every setup to its own target on the timeframe you entered. The first test = an intraday trade only, NOT the start of a swing (it will get shaken out). Only swing-trade after the second test has traded and shaken out the first. Trading the middle of a range instead of the original setup? Take the target — or you'll be shaken out of profit you already had.
- Bottom line: know the instrument's idiosyncrasies, trade only where expectation is positive (the second-test dip-in), and above all **don't over-leverage.**
