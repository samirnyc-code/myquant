**In short:** A new high/low tick that matches price is a **profit-taking signal, not an automatic reversal.** It's a **divergence** (trend continues) if price makes a further new extreme without a new tick; it's a **reversal** only once the trend breaks and confirms. This is Halsey's flagship tool (from a 2008 observation).

## Two kinds of tick extreme
- **Traditional / reversal:** high (or low) tick of the day that **matches price but breaks trend** and fails to make a new extreme.
- **Divergence:** tick + price make a new extreme together, then price **keeps making new extremes without a new matching tick** → the trend *continues*.

## The rules
- **Bullish divergence → stay long** until the next new **high** tick of the day. **Bearish → stay short** until the next new **low** tick. It's your confidence to hold a runner.
- A divergence lasts until tick and price make a new extreme **together** again.
- Every new high/low tick = a decision point: divergence continues, or it's ending.

## Divergence ending ≠ reversal (the key nuance)
- If a divergence **ends**, that's a **profit-taking** opportunity — every time. It is a **reversal only if the trend breaks and confirms** a new opposing series.
- After a high tick matches the high: **wait for the series of longs to fail** before shorting (mirror for lows). The **first opposing measured move** after that failure is the **best reversal entry** (highest R:R, highest participation).
- Then **trade the new series until the next extreme tick or the trend breaks.**

## Where it applies
- ES is the guide, but tradable on **YM and TF** too. There's often more opportunity in trend *failures* than in continuations.
