**In short:** The measured move IS the method: draw a Fib on a swing leg, enter the **50% (halfway-back)**, target **123% (−23.6%)**, and treat a **61.8% break as a trend change.** Markets go traditional → extension → expanded extension → trend break → reverse.

## The measured move
- Up leg (low→high): **50% = HWB entry**, **123% (−23.6%) = target**, **61.8% = failure/stop line.** Mirror for shorts.
- HFT programs trail price, rest limit orders at the 50%, and take profit at the 123% — you grab their coattails.

## The sequence (the diagram)
- **Traditional:** halfway-back pullback; chain into a **series** (target → pullback → next 50%).
- **Extension:** price blows *past* a 123% target without pulling back → redraw the 50% from the **last low → new high** (mirror for shorts). Looks like a traditional, different anchor.
- **Expanded extension:** when an extension blows past its *own* target too, the move accelerates (parabolic) — that's where the real runs happen.
- **Trend break / change:** a **61.8% failure** breaks the series → draw the **opposing** measured move; the stop is hit and the trend reverses.

## The anchor (the nuance you asked about)
- **Keep the same anchor** (to new highs) while extensions keep hitting target and pulling back.
- **Move the anchor to the last previous high** only when the move **expands past target** (extension-of-extension) — that's "eventually you change the anchor."
- **First extension off any brand-new anchor = observe only** (no limit). If it fails, the whole trend fails — so the stop sits just under it.
- **Full reset** on a trend break (new opposing measured move).

## Trading it
- 50% = entry, 123% = target, 61.8% = invalidation. Take profit on the timeframe you entered; trail the 61.8% through a series. Risk ≤1%, aim ≥2:1.
