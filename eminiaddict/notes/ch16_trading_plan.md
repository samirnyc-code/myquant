# Ch 16 — The Trading Plan (Keeping Yourself Safe with Statistical-Based Rules)

## Summary
Chapter 16 is THE ruleset chapter — the literal, codified trading plan. It opens with the "four legs of a trading strategy" (money management, entry rules, profit-taking rules, statistical-based rules), then presents the actual rules divided into four categories plus a setups-priority section and a journal mandate. Core recurring thresholds: **risk ≤1% per trade**; **no-trade zone 9:30–10:00 a.m. ET** (first half hour); **half size on Mondays, Fridays, options expiration, and rollover Thursdays/post-rollover Fridays**; **two US sessions 8:00–11:30 a.m. and 1:30–4:00 p.m. ET**; **no new trades after 3:45 p.m. ET**; **ES gap fills 5–10 points**, gap-fill entry window **8:00–8:30 a.m. EST**; **EUR/USD tradeable 3:00–11:30 a.m. ET**. Setups ranked: Daily (highest) → 15-minute → Micro (lowest).

## Key concepts & definitions
- **The Four Legs of a Trading Strategy** — like a four-legged chair; all four required or the strategy fails:
  - **Leg 1: Money Management** — "Risking 1 percent or less per trade is a rule that must never be broken." Casino vs gambler; use a position-sizing calculator every day before the bell.
  - **Leg 2: Entry Rules** — very specific prices; enter with confidence/conviction or you end up chasing. Failure to follow entry rules is caused by fear, which is caused by overleverage.
  - **Leg 3: Profit-Taking Rules** — taking profit is a planned event; always take profit at a profit target; if multi-contract, take partial; if single contract, close the trade. Greed rarely yields more profit. "Take what the market makes available to you and nothing more."
  - **Leg 4: Statistical-Based Trading Rules** — encapsulates entry/exit/money-management; developed by learning from mistakes via a **trading journal**.
- **Professional gap** — when the market opens **10 points** above/below the previous day's close.
- **No-trade zones** — 9:30–10:00 a.m. ET (open) and after 3:45 p.m. ET (close).
- **Two trading sessions (US):** 8:00–11:30 a.m. ET and 1:30–4:00 p.m. ET.
- **Intraday income trade vs longer-term wealth creation trade** — income closed same day; wealth trade lasts a week+ (EUR/USD favored for wealth trades).
- **Market breadth / NYSE Tick / Nasdaq Bank** — filters/timing tools for the NYSE session.

## Rules / setups / mechanics — VERBATIM, NUMBERED

Trading rules are divided into four categories (as stated on p182):
1. General Trading Account Rules
2. Gap Fill Rules
3. American (NYSE) Trading Rules
4. European (Euro) Trading Rules

### A. General Trading Account Rules
(Each rule below is the bolded lead sentence verbatim; explanatory text summarized.)

1. **"Never add money to the account."** — Start with the correct-size account and correct risk % from the beginning; needing to add money signals an unsolved problem.
2. **"Never place a trade without a stop loss."** — Stops should be automatic; with advanced trade management, stops can be specified before the trade is even filled.
3. **"Have a target for every trade."** — Every trade is planned before entry; without a target there is no way to know the risk-reward ratio, and without that "he has no business entering a trade."
4. **"Enter trades at MMs in the direction of the trend."** — "Go with the flow"/"trade the trend"; do not fight the major trend; let larger time frames show the path of least resistance.
5. **"Use limit orders."** — Be specific about the price you want; without a specific entry there is no trade and you don't know where your stop is.
6. **"Never risk more than 1 percent of total trading capital per trade."** — The most important rule; correct sizing makes ~90% of trader problems disappear.
7. **"Do NOT try to 'sell highs' and 'buy lows' just because the price is trading at a major support or resistance level. Wait for the technical failure in the most recent series of MMs."** — Don't pick tops/bottoms; wait for a trend break at a major S/R level (same rule the algorithms follow).
8. **"Do NOT rush a trade unless parameters are defined before the trade is placed."** — There's always another opportunity; don't chase; an unplanned trade should not be traded. "If you see the trade and you are not a part of it, it is over."
9. **"Focus on trading effectively and follow your rules."** — Don't focus on money; associate following rules with profit and breaking rules with losses.
10. **"Trade half position size on Mondays, Fridays, options expiration, Rollover Thursdays, and Fridays after the futures rollover."** — Fridays/Mondays are choppy, range-bound; Tuesdays–Thursdays are trending high-participation. If you cannot lower risk or trade a smaller contract on these days, consider not trading at all.

### B. Gap Fill Trading Rules

1. **"The best time to enter a gap fill trade is between 8 and 8:30 a.m. EST."**
2. **"E-mini S&P 500 Index (ES) gaps should be as least 5 points and not more than 10 points. If it doesn't meet the criteria, pass on the trade."** — Passing on gaps <5 points is a reward-not-worth-risk case.
3. **"If it's a professional gap and doesn't fill, then look for the first buy/sell signal in the direction of the gap."** — A professional gap = market opens 10 points above/below prior close. Trade the trend away from the gap fill and trail it until it fails; once that trend fails, trade the next MM in the direction of the gap fill.
4. **"Record unfilled gaps and keep their price level handy; the market will often fill open gaps later on in the week."** — Your record of unfilled gaps becomes valuable weeks/months later.
5. **"Stay in the trade until the gap fills or the stop is hit."** — Don't micromanage; a retest normally touches the entry again without stopping it out; maximize initial risk-reward by staying in until target hit or stopped.
6. **"Avoid gap plays on options expiration Fridays, rollover Thursdays, and the first trading day of the new month."** — But it's okay to play gap-up situations, as markets tend to run away and produce professional gap-and-goes.
7. **"After a narrow-range day, if the next day's gap is larger than the previous day's range, or the market's opening prices are outside the previous day's session high or low, stay away from a gap fill."** — Consolidation produces fuel for the gap; dangerous to fight the gap fill.
8. **Leader rule:** "The ES is the leader in the most technical market for the gap fill." — YM, TF (Russell), and NQ also have tradeable gap fills; use ES as a leading indicator and take profit on the other indices when the ES completes its gap fill (algorithms take profit on the others then, which can keep the others from fully filling).

### C. American (NYSE) Session Trading Rules

1. **"It is extremely important to only take the 50 percent setups that occur in 15-minute charts."** — Trading only 15-minute MMs filters trades and avoids overtrading; there may be only 5–7 MMs a day on 15-min (many more on smaller frames); 15-min MMs have better risk-reward. Trade the path of least resistance in the direction of larger time frames.
2. **"If market breadth is strong and increasing, look for only MM longs to trade."** — And the opposite in weak markets.
3. **"If market breadth is weak and decreasing, look for only MM shorts to trade."** — Trade the path of least resistance.
4. **NYSE Tick timing:** "The NYSE Tick chart helps us time our entries." — In a series of MM longs, look for **low ticks** to buy (anticipating long continuation); in a series of MM shorts, look for **high ticks** to sell.
5. **Second-test confirmation:** "If, for whatever reason, a trade fails to get initiated at the first test of an MM, check 'time and sales' to confirm participation in the second test." — The second test is the most dangerous test (initial participants under pressure); need a whole new group of participants to offset the potential shakeout.
6. **Nasdaq Bank confirmation:** "Another way to help traders filter the MMs they trade requires the Nasdaq Bank to confirm the move in the direction of the trade."
7. **Two sessions:** "Each trading day can be broken down into two trading sessions. The first trading session is from 8 to 11:30 a.m. ET. The second trading session is from 1:30 to 4 p.m. ET. The only exception is the no-trade zone between 9:30 and 10 a.m. ET. All trades must be initiated only during these two time frames." — Only exception is management of existing trades. Slow/low-volume times are the most dangerous.
8. **"It is very important to not initiate any new trades after 3:45 p.m."** — Last 15 minutes are among the most volatile (institutional end-of-day hedging via market orders); a no-trade zone similar to the open.
9. **End-of-day review:** "At the end of each day, it is important to review the day and the opportunities that the market made available." — Applying rules post-close increases emotional capital; congratulate yourself if you followed the rules.

### D. European (Euro) Session Trading Rules

1. **Two trade types:** Accounts grow from the **intraday income trade** (closed by end of day) and the **longer-term wealth creation trade** (a week or longer). The EUR/USD spot market and associated instruments are easier for longer-term wealth-creation trades.
2. **"The main goal in trading the currencies, specifically the EUR/USD, is to identify the trend and trade on the path of least resistance."** — Larger time frame = more important/powerful trend. No intraday NYSE tools available, so identifying the longer-term trend is critical; filter out all opportunities against the major trend.
3. **"The next goal is to categorize high-probability setups in terms of risk versus reward and place the highest priority on best trades."** — Lower risk-reward ratio = less priority (the contractor analogy: closest job = best risk-reward = wins; better R:R attracts more participants → higher likelihood of hitting target).
4. **Trading hours:** "Times to trade the EUR/USD are from 3:00 to 11:30 a.m. ET. Times not to trade or to trade slower (i.e., managing positions and stops) are any time after the European close at 11:30 a.m. ET." — After 11:30 a.m. ET the euro goes almost silent, often trading a **10-pip range** the rest of the day until the Asian session begins.

### E. Setups (priority ranking)

1. **Daily Setups** — highest priority; usually happen **once a week**; expect one each week completing its profit target (hitting a **−23 percent target**); highest-reward trades; can be identified **24 to 36 hours before** they happen.
2. **15-Minute Setups** — second-highest priority; taken with the trend on the daily charts; basis for most intraday trades; **three to seven MMs** can exist on this medium-term time frame during a single day.
3. **Micro Setups** — least priority; taken in the direction of daily + 15-minute setups; an entry tool into larger 15-min/daily trends; usable as income trades but risk overtrading, so use with caution.

### F. Trading Journal (mandate)
"The most important trading rule is to use a trading journal." Bulk-upload trades; analyze profitability down to minutes of the day, days of week, months of year. The chapter's rules (e.g., first-half-hour no-trade zone; restricting to 15-minute setups during the daily doldrums) came from years of journal data. Find where you lose money and add rules to narrow those weaknesses.

## Codifiable vs discretionary
**Codifiable (hard/objective):**
- All time windows (session bounds, no-trade zones, gap-entry window, EUR/USD hours).
- 1% risk cap; half-size day list.
- ES gap size band (5–10 pts); professional gap = 10 pts; gap-avoidance day list; narrow-range-day filter.
- 15-minute 50% setup selection; setup priority Daily > 15-min > Micro.
- Mandatory stop, mandatory target, limit-order entry.
- Daily-setup −23% target and 24–36h lead time.

**Discretionary / judgment:**
- Reading market breadth (strong/increasing vs weak/decreasing) to bias long/short.
- NYSE Tick timing (low/high ticks) and "time and sales" second-test confirmation.
- Nasdaq Bank confirmation.
- Ranking EUR/USD setups by risk-reward priority.
- "Wait for the technical failure in the most recent series of MMs" (structural judgment).
- Whether to trade at all on half-size days.
