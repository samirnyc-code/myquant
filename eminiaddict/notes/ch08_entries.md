# Ch 8 — Three Entry Strategies for Retracements

## Summary (3-5 lines)
This chapter defines the three rule-based entry strategies for entering a Measured Move (MM) retracement, which occur in the **same order every time, for every MM**: (1) the **First Test**, (2) the **Front Run of the Second Test**, and (3) the **Trend Break and Next MM**. All entries use limit orders placed in front of the 50% MM level ("front run"), filled first-come-first-served. The First Test is highest-probability/highest-volume; the Second Test is the riskiest but has the best risk-reward and must use smaller size/tighter stop; the Trend Break (breaking an opposing MM) is the confirmation of the support/resistance level and the profit target, launching a new series of MMs. Per-instrument front-run distances, stops, and targets are tabulated (ES: 2-tick front run, −6 tick stop, +2 first target, stop → −4 after first target).

## Key concepts & definitions
- **Goal**: A rule-based, consistent entry system. "Consistent rules produce consistent results."
- **Front run**: Placing a limit order a fixed number of ticks/pips *in front of* the 50% MM level so the limit order gets filled (there is always competition from programs at the first test). For ES the front run is **2 ticks** ("the front run that has worked on the ES futures for the past six years"); in a very aggressive rally/sell-off it could be 3 ticks.
- **First test**: The first pullback into the MM entry. Highest probability, most volume/participation, highest probability of hitting first target. Programs that identified the trend wait with limit orders in front of the MM.
- **Second test**: The retest of the first-test level. The **most dangerous test** — "Fade the first test, and go with the second" (trade the first test; don't trade the second, it could break out). If your first-test limit wasn't filled, sellers/buyers have "chased" the first test, and the retest risks a stop run.
- **Opposing MM**: An MM in the opposite direction. **Breaking the opposing MM = confirmation of a support/resistance level.** It confirms both the trend (a new pullback into it) and the profit target.
- **Reduced-risk (RR) trade**: After the first target is hit, the stop is moved to a tighter level (ES: −6 → −4), making the remaining position reduced-risk.
- **All-in/all-out**: The second-test entry is NOT an RR trade; it is taken all-in/all-out with smaller size (no scaling/RR management).

### Entry Strategy 1 — The First Test
- **Trigger**: Price pulls back into the first-test entry at the 50% MM. Place a limit order front-run in front of the MM (ES: 2 ticks) and wait. "The sooner the order is placed the better" — filled in order placed (first-come, first-served).
- **Mechanics (ES example, Fig 8.2 "Two-Tick Front Run")**: ES front run by 2 ticks. Normal ES stop = **6 ticks** (if market dips more than 6 ticks into the original entry → stopped out for a small loss). First target = **+2**. After first target hit, stop auto-moves from **−6 to −4** (reduced-risk trade).
- **Three rules for trading a first test**:
  1. **If you are not filled in the first test, immediately pull your limit order.** In aggressive trends the first test can be front run by very large distances; if unfilled, sellers have chased the first test, making the retest dangerous (potential stop run) — this is why the second test is the most dangerous. "Fade the first test, and go with the second."
  2. **Never chase an entry.** Wait for the next opportunity (second test entry).
  3. **If you are ever taken out of a first-test trade at breakeven, stop.** Prepare for the next entry opportunities: the front run of the second test, or wait for the trend break and next MM.

### Entry Strategy 2 — The Front Run of the Second Test
- **Trigger**: Front run the price that traded in the first test with a limit order, with a stop **right below the low in a long** (or right above the high in a short). Used if the first test was missed, if you weren't ready with a limit order, or if you didn't trust/were apprehensive of the first test (e.g., a news event or an opposing MM).
- **Character**: "By far the riskiest entry, but it also has the best risk-reward ratio." Because the second test is the most dangerous, it should be traded with **smaller size and a smaller stop**, and as an **all-in/all-out trade** (NOT an RR trade).
- **Mechanics (ES example, Fig 8.3)**: MM long that pulled back into its entry. Previous low on ES is **front run by 2 ticks** with a limit order. Stop is placed **2 ticks below the participation level** for a **total 4-tick stop**. Exact opposite for a series of MM shorts. Use limit orders — entries are very price-specific; sooner placed = better (first-come, first-served).
- **Three rules for trading second tests**:
  1. **If your limit order is not filled and price moves away, pull your limit order.** Aggressive trend does not give permission to chase with a market order.
  2. **Never chase an entry.** Wait for the next opportunity: a trend break entry and a next MM.
  3. **If you are ever stopped out of a trade, prepare for the next entry opportunity.**

### Entry Strategy 3 — The Trend Break and Next MM
- **Trigger / confirmation (capture exactly)**: The **trend break of an opposing MM** — "We call this a confirmation of a support or resistance level." Used if the first test was missed, if you didn't get filled in the front run of the second test, or if you didn't trust the second test. Setup context (Fig 8.4): the pullback has already traded its first test and moved away from entry, AND given a second-test entry that was front run and moved away (giving the trade room away from its entry). **Breaking the opposing MM tells us two things:**
  1. It confirms the trend and a new pullback into that trend.
  2. It confirms the profit target. (In the book's example, the target is **1,274.36**.)
  This begins a **new series of MM longs** moving away from the new long setup into the profit target.
- **Mechanics (Fig 8.4 trend break; Fig 8.5 next MM)**: The **next MM** on ES is **front run by 2 ticks** and uses its normal reduced-risk trading strategy. **Rule of thumb: the next MM can be traded with the same specifications as a first test** (Figure 8.1 values). The next MM is an excellent entry into the larger trend's target, with an excellent risk-reward ratio (entry and stop relatively small).
- **Three rules for trading next MMs**:
  1. **If your limit order is not filled and price moves away, pull your limit order.** No permission to chase with a market order.
  2. **Never chase an entry.** Wait for the next opportunity.
  3. **If you are ever stopped out of a next MM, stop.** The market is sloppy or the trend has not clearly been defined.

### The Progression of Entries (order is fixed — same every time)
1. **The first test will always be the best.** Program trades fight over the right to get filled. If you do not get filled, wait for the second test.
2. **The second test has a higher-risk entry into the trend.** Second tests should use tighter stops and reduced size. If you do not get filled, wait for the trend break and next MM.
3. **The opposing MM break confirms the target.** It gives permission to place a limit order and buy the next MM. If you do not get filled in the next MM, wait for the next setup to start all over again.

## Codifiable vs discretionary
**Codifiable:**
- Fixed 3-entry progression in strict order: First Test → Front Run of Second Test → Trend Break + Next MM.
- Per-instrument front run / stop / first target / adjusted stop (Figure 8.1 table).
- ES: front run +2 ticks, stop −6, first target +2, move stop −6→−4 after first target (RR).
- Second test (ES long): front run previous low by 2 ticks, stop 2 ticks below participation = 4-tick total stop, all-in/all-out, smaller size.
- Next MM = same specs as first test (Figure 8.1), front run 2 ticks, RR management.
- Hard order-management rules per entry (pull unfilled limit, never chase, stop after breakeven/stop-out).
- Trend break = break of the opposing MM → confirms trend + confirms target → start new MM series.
- Shorts are the exact mirror of longs.

**Discretionary:**
- Whether front run should be 2 vs 3 ticks in a "very aggressive rally or sell-off."
- Whether to "trust" a first or second test (news event, opposing MM, time-of-day conflict).
- Sizing on the second test ("smaller size," "smaller stop" — magnitude not specified).
- Judging when the market is "sloppy" or the trend "not clearly defined" (rule 3 of next MMs).
- Recognizing the opposing MM and where it breaks (the confirmation is structural/chart-read).
