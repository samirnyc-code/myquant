# Ch 1 — Today's Trading Environment (Descent of the Pit and Ascent of the Screen)

## Summary (3-5 lines)
Chapter 1 is context-setting, not method. It charts the shift from open-outcry pit trading to fully electronic/algorithmic markets, profiles who trades today (banks, funds, HFT firms, retail), and explains why order *size* matters (large orders move price). It introduces quant trading and HFT as the dominant forces and frames the book's thesis: a retail trader ("David") can still profit consistently against institutional "Goliaths" by using a sound, disciplined technical method that follows Smart Money. No measured-move mechanics appear here.

## Key concepts & definitions
- **Open outcry trading** = traditional pit floor trading with humans shouting buy/sell orders; being phased out.
- **Matching engine** = software running on an exchange's digital server that matches buy orders with sell orders prior to trade execution. East Coast engines mostly in New Jersey (Weehawken, Secaucus, Mahwah); Chicago exchanges host servers in Chicago.
- **Dark pools** = private exchanges accessible only to investors placing extremely large orders; allow huge orders to trade anonymously and hidden from public view until after execution, so price does not move against the trader.
- **Quant trading** = program trading based not on fundamentals but on quantitative analysis of technical data; practitioners = "quants."
- **High-frequency trading (HFT)** = platforms using extremely fast computers to place extremely quick trades based on algorithmic profit opportunities; "scalpers on steroids." Firms often colocate servers in the same buildings as exchange servers (New Jersey and Chicago) because a delay of a few milliseconds can decide a winning vs losing trade.
- **Smart Money / Big Players** = the large institutional participants whose "coattails" the book's strategy aims to ride.
- **Strategy vs tactics** = strategy is the overall plan to achieve the goal; tactics are the individual step-by-step techniques applied to execute the strategy. Book's goal = consistent profitability; strategy = follow the money / ride the Big Players / play as Smart Money does; tactics = the rest of the book.

## Rules / setups / mechanics
No trading rules, setups, entries, stops, or targets are defined in this chapter. Only quantitative/historical facts stated (quoted verbatim):
- 1997: more than 10,000 traders traded on pit floors.
- 2009: approximately 1,000 pit traders remained.
- Nasdaq emerged in 1971 as the world's first electronic stock market.
- CME founded 1898; opened **Globex** in 1992 (a 24-hour market for trading futures).
- London Stock Exchange (LSE) founded 1801; opened electronic trading market 1997.
- Toronto Stock Exchange (then TSE, now TSX) founded 1861; went electronic 1997.
- 2000: New York's International Securities Exchange (ISE) opened the first electronic options exchange.
- 2003–2004: Chicago's Citadel Investment Group launched its HFT system for U.S. equity options.
- 2006: NYSE (founded 1863) merged with Archipelago Exchange (ArcaEx), an ECN founded 1997.
- Estimates of quant trading share of daily market volume: **range from 60 percent to 80 percent or more**.
- Flash Crash date: **May 6, 2010**, attributed largely to algorithmic programs backing away en masse from the bid-ask spread → lack of demand → extremely rapid price drop.
- 2009 media: Goldman Sachs achieved record quarterly profits, much attributable to program trading.
- **31** of the largest HFT firms banded together into the **Principal Traders Group**.
- Core principle: large orders move the market — large buy orders (especially in series) cause price to rise; large sell orders (especially in series) cause price to fall (supply and demand).
- Cross-reference stated: Chapter 4 will show how tape-reading techniques help gauge presence/absence of large orders.
