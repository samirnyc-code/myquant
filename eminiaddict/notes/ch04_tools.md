# Ch 4 — More Tools for Trading Power (Filling the Toolbox)

## Summary (3-5 lines)
Chapter 4 adds timing and order-flow tools to the MM toolkit. It covers the calendar (seasonal volume patterns, witching/expiration days, FOMC/ECB/Jackson Hole/Davos events, day-of-week personalities) and the clock (news-event times, global market-hour overlaps, intraday reversal periods and no-trade zones). It then covers reading the tape (time & sales, T&S) for market speed and buy/sell pressure, and the DOM (depth of market / ladder) both as a deceptive ("liars' poker") internal and as an essential order-entry/management tool. Sizing rules (half size / full size / SOH) recur throughout.

## Key concepts & definitions
- **SOH (Sit On Hands)**: deliberately not trading; "not trading at all is a trade decision." Truism: "there will always be another trading opportunity."
- **Trade at half size**: trade half the normal number of contracts/shares (4→2, 8→4).
- **Trade the reaction, not the news**: "Some traders 'trade the news.' MM traders trade the reaction to the news." Content of news is unimportant; the market's reaction is.
- **Triple witching**: Fridays in **March, June, September, December** when S&P futures, S&P 100 index options, and individual stock options all expire. Last hour = "triple witching hour" (high volume, wild gyrations).
- **Quadruple witching**: adds single-stock futures contracts; **March** quad witching can be wildest.
- **Options expiration**: Friday of the third week of each month.
- **The doldrums**: New York lunchtime, **12 p.m. to 2 p.m.** ET — extremely lethargic, low-volume trading; a no-trade / trade-with-extreme-caution zone.
- **No-trade zone (NY open)**: first half-hour of the NY session, **9:30 a.m. to 10 a.m.** ET, for major-index-related contracts (gap-trading zigzag).
- **11:30 reversal period**: euro/currency reversal as European traders take profit (LSE closes 11:30 a.m. ET, London Forex 12 p.m. ET). Followed by a "currency dead zone" of little activity beginning 12 p.m. ET.
- **2 p.m. post-doldrums reversal**: reversal often occurs at the end of the doldrums (~2 p.m.); special case on **Mondays** when institutions sit out until 2 p.m. then enter weekly positions.
- **Frankfurt/London reversal**: a direction started at the Frankfurt open is often reversed at the London open (EUR/USD, 6E).
- **Time & Sales (T&S) / "the tape")**: window displaying each trade just after execution — time (HH:MM:SS), price, size. Color: trade below previous = red (seller); above previous = green (buyer); unchanged = same color as previous.
- **DOM (Depth of Market) / "ladder"**: displays bid and ask prices at various levels with the size bid/offered at each; shows price and size of each trade just after execution.
- **Flashing the DOM**: Big Money entering a large bid/offer away from best bid/ask then pulling it before it can execute, to lure others ("liars play poker"). HFT has honed this.
- **Market speed / tempo**: judged by the rate trades flow through T&S; dictates trade cadence.

## Rules / setups / mechanics
- **Day-of-week sizing rules of thumb**:
  1. Trade **half size on Mondays**, especially in the morning after the NYSE open.
  2. Trade **full size Tuesday through Thursday**.
  3. Trade **half size on Fridays, or not at all.**
- **Day personalities**: Monday sets weekly direction (subdued/sluggish, low AM volume). Tuesday action begins in earnest (volume up, more "technical" adherence to MM levels); continues Wed–Thu. Friday = "wacky Friday" — swift unexpected spikes/dips especially after lunch in New York; institutions close weekly positions (esp. before three-day weekends).
- **Low-volume seasonal calendar**:
  - U.S. trading "year begins in September"; last week of August exceptionally low volume, often stretching past Labor Day until the **September FOMC meeting**.
  - Robust volume until Christmas holidays; brief lull the day after Thanksgiving.
  - Last week of December (Christmas→New Year's) = relatively low activity; picks up after Jan 1.
  - Seasonally strong until "Sell in May and Go Away."
  - Nadir the **week after July 4**.
  - In low volume: **trade half size or not at all.**
- **Witching statistic (past 20 years)**: the week before quadruple witching has seen upward price movement **70 percent** of the time; the following week has seen downward price movement **65 percent** of the time.
- **FOMC**: one of **eight** regularly scheduled meetings per year; announcements usually occur **around 2:15 p.m. ET**; futures move violently for many minutes after — "Trading during this time period is gambling, not trading." SOH.
- **News-event rule**: almost always best to SOH during a market-moving news event. If in a profitable trade, either exit well before the event or ride it through. If not in a trade, don't enter during the event or its immediate wake; wait for a clear direction before a postevent trade.
- **Global overlaps (high-volume windows)**: **2 a.m.–4 a.m.** ET (Asian + European) and **8 a.m.–12 p.m.** ET (European + American); the latter especially has the greatest volume/liquidity → best for MM trading.
- **Gap / premarket trading & NY-open no-trade zone**: gap traders work the premarket (8 a.m. ET for East Coasters) targeting gap fill / half-gap fill; first half-hour after NY open is zigzag as Big Money closes at various fills. Rule: "If you're not in a trade entered in the premarket or earlier, SOH." Corollary: **if the gap fills before 10 a.m., the no-trade zone is no longer in effect.**
- **Market-speed truisms/hints**:
  - "When the market's slow, trade it slow; when it's fast, trade it fast."
  - Waiting **30 minutes or more** for an initial profit target = slow tempo.
  - Fast markets: trade setups come in intervals of **7 to 10 minutes**; slow markets: intervals elongate to **30 minutes or more**.
  - All price movement is cause and effect (slow cause → slow effect).
  - In slow markets, quick explosive moves are stops being hit (market orders). Cardinal rule: **"Never buy highs or sell lows."**
  - If you see significant speed in a micro MM, step back to the next larger time frame for confirmation before trading.
- **DOM rules**:
  - "Don't believe that unusually large bids or offers on the DOM are what they appear to be because they probably aren't" (flashing).
  - Despite deception, a sophisticated DOM-based order-entry/management system (automating stop and target placement/movement) is essential. "Get a good DOM before entering a single trade." Absence of a sophisticated DOM = "an extreme impediment" to profitability.
- **Tape-reading**: read T&S from the bottom up; topmost entry = last trade executed. Judge whether more red (selling) or green (buying) and the relative size of blocks; interpret only in context of the preceding 15–30 minutes.

## Codifiable vs discretionary
- **Codifiable**:
  - Day-of-week sizing: half Mon, full Tue–Thu, half/none Fri.
  - Time windows: doldrums 12–2 p.m.; NY-open no-trade zone 9:30–10 a.m. (voided if gap fills before 10); FOMC ~2:15 p.m.; overlaps 2–4 a.m. and 8 a.m.–12 p.m.; 11:30 reversal; 2 p.m. reversal; currency dead zone from 12 p.m.
  - Witching schedule (3rd-Friday Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) + 70%/65% pre/post-quad-witching stats.
  - FOMC = 8 meetings/year.
  - Market-hours table (Table 4.1).
  - Setup-interval heuristics (7–10 min fast; 30+ min slow).
  - T&S color rules (red = down/seller, green = up/buyer).
  - Rule "never buy highs or sell lows."
- **Discretionary**:
  - Reading tape speed/pressure and block sizes → judging tempo.
  - Interpreting DOM (distinguishing real vs flashed size).
  - Deciding when a "clear direction" has emerged post-news.
  - Whether to ride a trade through news or exit early.
  - Reacting to reversal periods (treated as "watch out for" zones, not hard signals).
