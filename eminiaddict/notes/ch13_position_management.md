# Ch 13 — How to Manage Positions and Take Profits

## Summary
Halsey argues that taking profit is at least as important as entries: "There are a million ways to get into a trade. A systematic strategy for taking profit is in many ways much more important than an entry strategy." Because measured-move (MM) targets in wide-range markets can be so large they'd require holding overnight (with overnight margin and asleep-risk), the chapter gives **four ways to take profits inside a series of MMs**, letting a trader capture pieces of a big move while staying flat by the close. The governing rule is **"take profit on the time frame you entered the trade on,"** and each MM has its own unique entry and target. The four profit-taking methods are: (1) the **Distance Formula** (fast scalp; target = distance between the 50% and 38.2% lines), (2) **Trailing the series** (trail 61.8% levels through traditional→extension MMs until a 61.8% fails), (3) **After the confirmation of trend** (take partial profit when an opposing MM breaks), and (4) the **−23% profit target** (intraday swing trades; the −23% is the exact reversal point at which a market turns). Overarching discipline theme: systematic exits kill greed; "No trader has ever gone broke taking profit"; "There is no such thing as a missed trend."

## Key concepts & definitions
- **The Rule for Measured Moves**: "Each and every MM has its own unique entry and target: **take profit on the time frame you entered the trade on.**"
- **−23 percent profit target**: "the exact reversal point at which a market turns." Used for intraday swing trades when a setup is too big to be hit by end of day.
- **Distance Formula**: a small scalp-type trade; the **fastest** way to take profit; for short-term income-type trades. Target distance = distance between the **50% and the 38.2%** retracement lines. Target size scales with MM size (larger MM → larger target). Can also serve as the **first target** in a scaled-out trade.
- **Trailing the series**: trailing **61.8% levels** to stay in the trend and maximize the risk-reward ratio, out to an MM's −23% target.
- **Traditional MMs vs. Extension MMs**: progression runs traditional MMs first; after price moves past its profit targets it begins trading **extensions**; you then trail the 61.8% of the extension MMs. Progression = traditional → extension → (if extensions fail) trend fails.
- **Confirmation of trend**: taking "small bites" of bigger MMs via partial profit; confirms the larger trend.
- **Opposing MM**: "a trade setup that trades against or in opposition to the major trend." Its **break** is a profit-taking opportunity that (a) confirms the main trend's profit target and (b) gives a next-MM entry.
- **Next MM**: after an opposing MM breaks (a trend break), the next MM can be a brand-new position or an addition to the active one, and can be held to the larger MM's target.
- **Fear and greed framing**: systematic entries keep fear in check; taking profit at each MM's unique target keeps greed in check.

## Rules / setups / mechanics

### The Rule for Measured Moves
- Each and every MM has its own unique entry and target.
- **Take profit on the time frame you entered the trade on.**

### The four ways to take profits inside a series of MMs
1. The distance formula
2. Trailing the series
3. After the confirmation of trend
4. The −23 percent profit target

### 1. The Distance Formula (exact)
- **Target distance = the distance between the 50% line and the 38.2% line.** (Entry is at the 50%; instead of using the −23% target, use the 38.2% line as the profit target.)
- Fastest way to take profit; small scalp-type / short-term income trade.
- Target size depends on MM size (larger MM → larger target); it takes the range/volatility of the day into account.
- Can be used as the **first target** on a scaled-out trade; after the first target is hit, the **stop is moved** (standard scaling-out); the first target gives room to avoid being shaken out on a retest of entry.
- **Best used**: Friday afternoons and Monday mornings; the **daily doldrums 11:30 a.m.–1:30 p.m. ET**; slow/choppy markets; and as a first target in **extremely volatile** markets (wide ranges that could stop you at original entry).
- **Downside**: unless trading multiple contracts, the move to the full −23% target is missed (solution = use it as first target of a scaled trade — Fig 13.2).
- Figure examples: **Fig 13.1** distance between 50% and 38.2% = **20 pips** of profit (and price traded that distance **three times** off its 50% line during a slow market — profiting on the same trade multiple times). **Fig 13.2** distance to first target = **12 pips**.
- Video: "The Distance Formula," http://eminiaddict.com/?p=5352.

### 2. Trailing a Series of Measured Moves
Rules:
- Trail the series of MMs until the **61.8% level fails** (maximizes entry + risk-reward).
- Respect the **normal progression of MMs**: trend trades traditional MMs first; traditional MMs continue until the trend breaks the 61.8% level **or** blows past the trade's profit targets. After moving past its profit targets, price trades **extensions**; now trail the **61.8% level of the MM extensions**. Progression: traditional MMs → extension MMs → if extensions fail, the trend fails.
- **If the series fails a 61.8% level, profit must be taken.**
- Figure example: **Fig 13.3** — MM short with a target **300 pips** away; trail traditional MM's 61.8% to maximize; trade moves past profit target → new MM extension → trail extension's 61.8% → break of extension's 61.8% ends the trend → profit taken (a "broken series").

### 3. After the Confirmation of Trend
- Technique for taking **partial profit** and confirming a larger trend ("small bites of bigger MMs").
- Between an MM's 50% retracement entry and its −23% profit target are opportunities to take partial profit; can also **add** to a trade or **enter a new position**.
- **When an opposing MM breaks, two things happen simultaneously:**
  1. The main trend **confirms its profit target**.
  2. A **next MM** is given as an entry opportunity.
- The next MM (Fig 13.5) can be a brand-new position or an addition; it can be **held to the larger MM's target**.

### 4. The −23 Percent Profit Target (take profit on the time frame you entered)
- For **intraday swing trades**; use when a setup is **too big to be hit by end of day**, or when intraday profits matter more than a swing/long-term position.
- The −23% is the **exact reversal point at which a market turns**.
- Each MM has its own unique target. **If ever in doubt about taking profit — always take profit.** "There is no such thing as a missed trend"; there will always be another retracement/entry.
- Being flat lets the trader look for the next MM.

### Summary heuristics (which method when — verbatim guidance)
- **Distance formula** → short-term income trades; slow/choppy markets; **Friday afternoons and Monday mornings**.
- **Trailing the series** → when the market is **trending**; **Tuesday through Thursday** markets are usually in trends.
- **Confirmation of trend** → extended ranges / large setups requiring smaller bites; use opposing MMs that fail to confirm trends, take partial profit.
- **Traditional −23%** → intraday swing trades; each MM has its own unique target; "No trader has ever gone broke taking profit."

## Codifiable vs discretionary

**Codifiable (mechanical, testable):**
- Distance formula target = |50% line − 38.2% line| (exact, computable from MM retracement levels).
- Use distance-formula value as first target; move stop after first target hit (scaling rule).
- Trail rule: trail 61.8% level; when a 61.8% level fails, take profit.
- Progression: traditional MM 61.8% → extension MM 61.8% → fail = exit.
- −23% target as the swing/EOD-too-big target and defined reversal point.
- Opposing-MM break → confirm target + next-MM entry (two mechanical consequences).
- Time-of-day/day-of-week mappings: distance formula on Fri PM / Mon AM / 11:30–1:30 ET doldrums; trailing Tue–Thu trends.

**Discretionary (judgment):**
- Identifying MM structure, 50%/38.2%/61.8% levels, and what counts as an "opposing MM" or a valid "series."
- Choosing which of the four methods to apply on a given day (guidance is heuristic, not strict).
- "Take profit on the time frame you entered" requires knowing/defining the entry time frame.
- Deciding partial-profit sizes / whether to add vs. take profit.
- Recognizing when a trend "fails" vs. temporary noise beyond the stated 61.8% rule.
