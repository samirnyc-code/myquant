# Ch 15 — The Inner Trader (The Emotions of a Trader)

## Summary
Chapter 15 is the trading-psychology chapter, but embedded in it is the book's core objective model of price: the **measured move (MM)** and its structural cycle. Psychology thesis: think like the **casino, not the gambler** — trade a statistical edge with sound money management and specific rules; poor money management drives high emotion and bad decisions, correct sizing removes the fear. The objective core: markets move ~90% of the time in reliable, logical measured moves and ~10% in unreliable/reactive noise (news). Each MM runs through **four phases** built around Fibonacci levels (50% entry, 61.8% defense line, 100% target, −23.6% overshoot). A series of MMs **progresses** from traditional → extension → expanded extension ("traditionals, to extensions, to straight up"). Two events govern everything: (1) a trade hits target and doesn't stop = extension begins; (2) a trade breaks its 61.8% line = trend fails.

## Key concepts & definitions
- **Casino mentality vs gambler mentality** — trader-as-casino wins over time via statistical edge + money management + rules; gambler is undercapitalized and over-risking. "The few take advantage of the many."
- **Emotional capital** — the confidence a trader has in his system and rules; gained only by executing the plan successfully day after day; can be more important than account size and is fragile (a bad decision/loss damages it).
- **Trading well vs trading for profit** — focus on executing rules perfectly ("to a tee"), not on the dollar amount. Some traders switch their DOM display from dollars to ticks to remove emotion.
- **Proactive vs reactive** — professionals are proactive (planned MMs, in the trade early, moving to target); inexperienced traders are reactive (chase moves already underway). "If you see the move and you're not in it, the trade is over." "If a trade isn't planned, it isn't rated." Refuse to trade an unplanned MM.
- **90/10 rule** — ~90% of the time the market trades in predictable, logical MMs; the other ~10% is unreliable (driven by news/reactive trades). Also: ~90% of volume is traded by computers/algorithms.
- **Two equations:** Poor Money Management + High Levels of Emotion = Bad Decisions; Strong Money Management + Controlled Emotions = Good Decisions.
- **Daily review exercise** — every day after the close, go back to the charts and review the opportunities the day presented; builds emotional capital.
- **10,000 hours** — cites Gladwell's *Outliers*; "perfect practice makes perfect" — consistent rule-following → consistent results.

## Rules / setups / mechanics — the objective MM model

### The Phases of Measured Moves — "The Four Phases"
Each MM goes through four phases that repeat until the cycle breaks; when broken, the four phases begin in the opposite direction.
1. **Phase 1** — entry into the trade at the **50 percent MM**. Phase 1 must stay within the **61.8 percent level** to continue its cycle.
2. **Phase 2** — time in the trade between the entry and the previous high or low of the MM.
3. **Phase 3** — time in the trade between the previous high/low and the profit target of the MM.
4. **Phase 4** — the profit target of the entire MM. Phase 4 stops at the MM's profit target and retraces into the next MM in the series.

- **Signal for continuation of the series** = defense (hold) of the **61.8 percent line** inside the next MM.
- **Signal for trend reversal** = **break of the 61.8 percent line**. The moment a Phase-1 entry fails to reach target and breaks 61.8%, trend flips (buyers→sellers or sellers→buyers); the old cycle ends and a new cycle repeats until the next 61.8% failure.
- Bullish MMs continue as long as buyers defend 61.8% (demand > supply → price rises); mirror for bearish (supply > demand → price declines).

### The Series of Measured Moves — the progression
Mantra: **"The markets trade from traditionals, to extensions, to straight up."**

**Basic / Traditional MM** — series begins here. A traditional MM continues until one of three things happens:
1. Hits its target and retraces into the next traditional MM.
2. Fails a 61.8 percent level.
3. Breaks out of a profit target → it becomes an **extension**.

**Extension MM** — continues until one of three things happens:
1. Hits its target and retraces into the next extension MM. *If the extension does not break out of its profit target, the same most-recent-low is used for the next extension.*
2. Fails a 61.8 percent level.
3. Breaks out of a profit target → starts a **new extension**.

**Expanded Extension MM** — general rule: "The market must continue to go straight up in extensions, or not at all. If extensions break, the trend breaks." Continues until one thing happens:
1. An expanded extension **fails a 61.8 percent level**.

### The Progression / Flow Chart — the two triggers
From Figure 15.3 (the MM flow chart), a trader's **two triggers** are:
1. A trade **hits its profit target and does not stop** → an extension begins.
2. A trade **breaks its 61.8 percent level** and fails its trend.
"Everything that changes in the market revolves around these two events."

## Codifiable vs discretionary
**Codifiable (objective):**
- Entry at the **50%** retracement of the MM.
- **61.8%** as the hard invalidation / trend-defense line (hold = continue, break = reverse).
- **100%** = profit target; **−23.6%** = overshoot/next-entry zone.
- Classification traditional → extension → expanded extension by whether price breaks the prior profit target.
- The two triggers (target-and-no-stop → extension; 61.8% break → trend fail).
- Retracement geometry per move type (Fig 15.2): outside-of-swing vs same-side-of-swing pulls.

**Discretionary / psychological:**
- Everything in the "Mentality / Money Management / Emotions / Attitude / Trading Well" sections (casino mindset, emotional capital, reducing size when emotional, daily chart review).
- Judgment of when price action is in the "90% reliable" regime vs the "10% news noise" regime.
