ES Second Entries × Regime: the raw setup is dead — the execution around it wasn't

Five years of tick data (1,231 sessions, 2021–2026), the validated tick-driven regime machine, and 3,340 mechanical second entries. The signal loses money in every regime. Five execution and context changes — none of them the entry pattern itself — flip it to a book that survives a train/test split, a 160-cell parameter sweep, and a strict-fill audit.

§1What was on the table

Three locked components, none invented for this study:

  • Regime — the tick-driven phase machine (v4), the engine validated bar-by-bar on the 2022-02-24 and 2022-04-12 reference days. Every price change advances the state: BULL / NEUTRAL / BEAR, day-scoped.
  • Setups — the S61 entry engine (spec locked 2026-07-08): strict legs, tick-resolved outside bars, origin-reset entry counting. The second entry (2EL/2ES) is Brooks' H2/L2: the second attempt to resume after a pullback.
  • Fills — everything tick-simulated. $5 round turn, 1 ES contract, flat at session end.

Base result, before any changes: T1 book PF 0.82 (−$108k), T2 book PF 0.78 (−$174k) — negative in all six regime × direction cells. The regime filter alone moves PF by at most 0.05. The entry pattern, mechanized naively, has no edge on ES.

§3The five levers that worked

  1. Stop chasing the breakout — retest limit entry. The base sim buys a stop-entry one tick beyond the break with a tick of slip. Placing a limit 4 ticks (1 pt) back from the trigger instead — and skipping the trade if it never pulls back — recovered $29 of the $32/trade deficit and still filled 96% of signals. This is the single biggest finding: the entry pattern was roughly fair value; the chase was the loss.
  2. Time of day. Entries in the last ~90 minutes (machine-hours 14–15) lost $90k of the −$174k base deficit (PF 0.43 and 0.07): they cannot reach a target before the forced EOD exit. The 08 bucket is thin and terrible. Tradeable window: 09:00–13:59 machine-clock.
  3. Wide fixed stop. The classic signal-bar+1-tick stop gets probed out; a fixed 4-pt stop dominates it everywhere (Brooks cell: PF 0.94 → 1.13 at 2R just from the stop change).
  4. Hold to the close. EOD-hold beats every fixed target in every scope tested. The 2E's payoff is trend-day drift, not a 2R scalp.
  5. With-trend + efficient day. Phase-machine with-trend only (long in BULL, short in BEAR), optionally plus ER10 (10-bar efficiency ratio) in its top half. ER, ADX and EMA-extension were the only monotonic features in the scan — all say the same thing: this trade pays only when the day is trending efficiently.

§4How to find the signal

The edge spec — ES 5-minute RTH, day-scoped.
  1. Track the regime (phase machine): the day opens NEUTRAL. A tick above the latest swing-high pivot while a higher-low exists → BULL; mirror for BEAR; a tick through the standing major HL/LH ends the trend → NEUTRAL. On a chart: shade the day by these states; you only care about the shaded trend spans.
  2. Count entries (S61 origin-reset counting): in a pullback against the trend, the first bar whose extreme is taken out in the trend direction is the 1st entry; if the pullback resumes and a second break fires, that is the second entry — the signal. This is Brooks' H2/L2, counted mechanically.
  3. Qualify it: regime must be with-trend (2EL in BULL, 2ES in BEAR) and the clock inside the 09–13 machine-hour window. Optional upgrade, historically the strongest cell: ER10 ≥ ~0.20 (top half). Longs have carried most of the edge.
  4. Do not chase. When the trigger fires, place a limit 1 pt back from the trigger price. No fill = no trade (≈4% of signals run away; let them).
  5. Risk: fixed 4-pt stop from fill. No target — hold to the session close, exit EOD flat. Know the profile going in: ~19% winners — 80% of trades die at the small stop (−$205), and nearly every trade that survives to the close is a winner (96%), averaging +$1,046. This is a right-tail harvest, psychologically brutal and mechanically simple.

The machine-hour window is this machine's clock, not CT — map it against exchange timestamps before trading it live.

§9What would kill it — read before believing

Fills. The limit-entry edge is bracketed, not known: touch-fills say {{NET_TCH}}$, strict through-fills say {{NET_THR}}$. Live queue position will land in between. If real fills track the strict model's worst year (2024: PF 0.90), the ER filter or long-only is load-bearing.

Selection pressure. Five levers were chosen from many tested — the train/test split, the parameter plateau, and every-year-positive mitigate but do not eliminate selection bias. The strict-fill 2024 dip is the honest wart.

Sizing. Verified on ES at $5 RT (≈$33/trade edge). At MES scale, commissions decide survival — §8 quantifies exactly which books and commission levels clear the bar.

Timezone. All session hours are machine-clock. Map to exchange time before live use.

§10Is this programmable?

Yes — every rule in the spec is causal and mechanical. Nothing requires judgment: the regime is a deterministic state machine over price changes, the entry count is a deterministic bar/tick rule, and entry, stop, and exit are order types every platform supports.

  • The regime machine was built for this. The Python engine is an explicit emulation of an NT8 indicator running Calculate.OnPriceChange — the tick loop body is the intended C# handler. Porting it is transcription, not research.
  • Order logic is plain ATM material: when a qualifying 2E triggers → submit a limit 1 pt back from the trigger, working until filled or session cutoff; on fill → attach a fixed 4-pt stop; cancel/flatten at the session close; no entries after the 13:59 machine-hour boundary (map to exchange time first).
  • ER10 filter is ten closes of arithmetic — trivial in any indicator.
  • The honest gap: backtest limit fills are modeled (touch vs through brackets it); a live implementation should log actual fill rates vs the model before scaling — that log settles the one number the backtest cannot.
